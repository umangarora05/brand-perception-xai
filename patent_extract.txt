ABSTRACT CONTEXT-ADAPTIVE EXPLAINABLE ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE SYSTEM FOR BRAND-LOGO
TRUST-PROPENSITY INFERENCE AND AUTOMATED DESIGN PRESCRIPTION A method for context-adaptive brand-
logo trust-propensity inference includes extracting multi-domain features (130) from a logo image, mapping a colour vector to
emotion-labelled palettes (150), generating a trust-propensity coefficient (160), generating raw feature attributions (170),
classifying a chromatic operating condition and producing a context-calibrated attribution vector while preserving the raw
attribution vector at a Dynamic Signal Calibration Module (180), generating a quantitative design prescription (190), and
outputting the trust-propensity score and design prescription (210).

FIELD OF INVENTION 
[0003] 
The present disclosure relates to explainable artificial intelligence 
systems for computer vision and brand-identity analytics, and more particularly to 
a context-adaptive system for inferring a trust-propensity coefficient from a brand-
10 
logo image, generating chromatic-condition-dependent feature attributions, and 
producing quantitative design prescriptions. 
BACKGROUND 
[0004] 
The following text of background art is provided for purposes of 
understanding and is not an admission that any cited document or technique forms 
15 
part of the prior art under applicable law. 
[0005] 
Brand logos communicate identity through interacting chromatic, 
geometric and typographic attributes. Existing logo-processing systems primarily 
recognise, retrieve, classify or generate logos. Design evaluation remains dependent 
on human opinion, focus groups or general-purpose feature-importance tools that 
20 
apply the same interpretation logic to every image. Uniform analysis creates 
misleading output in at least two situations: a monochromatic logo may receive an 
irrelevant colour recommendation, and an intentionally dominant brand colour may 
be treated as a defect merely because its raw model attribution is negative. 
Conventional explainability also identifies influential features without determining 
25 
a bounded, actionable modification capable of improving the predicted outcome. 
[0006] 
US10769496B2, titled "Logo Detection" and assigned to Adobe Inc., 
discloses a system that receives an image, detects candidate logo regions, extracts 
neural feature vectors and compares them with reference-logo vectors to identify a 
logo. This reference addresses logo recognition and localisation but does not infer 
30 
3 
a trust-propensity coefficient, map a logo to an emotion manifold, adapt 
explanations to chromatic conditions, or generate a quantitative design prescription. 
[0007] 
US10558758B2, titled "Enhancing a Computer to Match Emotion and 
Tone in Text with the Emotion and Tone Depicted by the Color in the Theme of the 
Page or Its Background" and assigned to International Business Machines 
5 
Corporation, associates emotion or tone derived from text with page-theme or 
background colour. This reference processes text and page colour rather than logo-
image geometry, typography and learned visual representations. The reference does 
not perform matched-pair logo analysis, logo trust inference or explainability-
driven redesign. 
10 
[0008] 
US10467526B1, titled "Artificial Intelligence System for Image 
Similarity Analysis Using Optimized Image Pair Selection and Multi-Scale 
Convolutional Neural Networks" and assigned to Amazon Technologies, Inc., uses 
selected image pairs and multi-scale convolutional neural networks for image-
similarity analysis. This reference measures general image similarity but does not 
15 
use a controlled baseline/revision logo pair to isolate a design intervention, does not 
derive a psychographic trust target, and does not generate context-calibrated 
attributions or logo-design prescriptions. 
[0009] 
Peer-reviewed literature has established that colour affects trust 
judgements, that logo naturalness and colour influence brand-gender perception and 
20 
affective response, and that perceived logo colourfulness influences consumer 
judgements through perceived product variety and brand positioning. However, 
these studies provide consumer-behaviour evidence rather than an automated logo-
analysis architecture. None of the reviewed references discloses matched-pair 
differential inference, adaptive explanation routing based on chromatic conditions, 
25 
or quantitative design prescription. 
[0010] 
The present disclosure addresses the above shortcomings of the prior 
art. However, the subject matter disclosed herein is entirely different from the prior 
art in terms of novelty and technological advancements.

FIG. 1 illustrates an overall architecture of a Differential Trust Engine 
100 according to various embodiments. The Differential Trust Engine 100 may 
operate in two distinct input modes: a single-logo mode where a single logo image 
is analyzed independently, and a matched-pair mode where a baseline logo r0 and 
30 
9 
a controlled revision r1 are processed together to isolate the effect of a deliberate 
design intervention. 
[0040] 
An Image Input and Validation Module 110 may receive either a single 
logo image or a matched baseline/revision pair. The Image Input and Validation 
Module 110 may verify file type, dimensions, transparency and integrity of the 
5 
input. In some cases, the Image Input and Validation Module 110 validates that the 
received logo image conforms to supported formats and resolution requirements 
before passing the validated input to subsequent processing stages. 
[0041] 
An Image Preprocessing Module 120 may perform resizing, 
normalisation, colour-space conversion, background handling and optional 
10 
foreground segmentation on the received logo images. The Image Preprocessing 
Module 120 may convert the logo image to a standardised representation suitable 
for feature extraction. 
[0042] 
A Multi-Domain Feature Extraction Module 130 may extract chromatic 
features including colour statistics, saturation and dominant-channel ratio from the 
15 
logo image. The Multi-Domain Feature Extraction Module 130 may extract 
geometric features including contrast, circularity, symmetry, stroke characteristics, 
edge density and spatial balance from the logo image. The Multi-Domain Feature 
Extraction Module 130 may extract typography-related descriptors from the logo 
image. The Multi-Domain Feature Extraction Module 130 may extract a learned 
20 
image embedding using a neural network encoder from the logo image. In some 
cases, the Multi-Domain Feature Extraction Module 130 may use ResNet50 as an 
exemplary neural network encoder for learned image embeddings. 
[0043] 
A Matched-Pair Differential Encoder 140 may compute differential 
features when a matched pair is supplied. The Matched-Pair Differential Encoder 
25 
140 may compute an embedding difference ΔE = E(r1) − E(r0) between baseline 
and revised logo embeddings. The Matched-Pair Differential Encoder 140 may 
compute an embedding displacement d = ||ΔE||₂ as a displacement feature between 
the baseline and revised logos. The Matched-Pair Differential Encoder 140 may 
retain engineered feature differences Δf to support feature-specific interpretation of 
30 
10 
the design intervention, capturing typography, stroke weight, spacing, geometry or 
colour modifications. 
[0044] 
An Emotion-Manifold Mapping Module 150 may map a dominant or 
representative colour vector c to k neighbouring palette vectors pj using nearest-
neighbour search. The Emotion-Manifold Mapping Module 150 may calculate an 
5 
affective target as a distance-weighted combination of emotion dimensions 
associated with neighbouring palettes. The Emotion-Manifold Mapping Module 
150 may implement nearest-neighbour search through a KD-tree or equivalent 
indexing structure. In some cases, the Emotion-Manifold Mapping Module 150 
may use a corpus of 745 palettes tagged across 35 psychological dimensions, 
10 
expanded to 3,725 palette-associated records. 
[0045] 
A Trust-Inference Engine 160 may generate a normalised trust-
propensity coefficient using a gradient-boosted decision-tree model. In some cases, 
the Trust-Inference Engine 160 may use LightGBM as an exemplary gradient-
boosted decision-tree implementation. The Trust-Inference Engine 160 receives the 
15 
extracted features and mapped affective target to produce a preliminary trust-
propensity coefficient. 
[0046] 
An Ensemble Corroboration Module 165 may combine outputs from a 
differential model Td and a general-logo model Tg using the formula Tf = αTd + 
(1−α)Tg with configurable weights. In some cases, the Ensemble Corroboration 
20 
Module 165 may use an illustrative laboratory configuration with α = 0.60 for 
combining differential and general-logo model outputs. 
[0047] 
An Explainability Engine 170 may generate raw local feature-
attribution values for the current prediction using model-agnostic explanation 
techniques. In some cases, the Explainability Engine 170 may use SHAP as an 
25 
exemplary technique for generating raw local feature-attribution values. 
[0048] 
A Dynamic Signal Calibration Module 180 may classify the chromatic 
operating condition as achromatic, ordinarily chromatic, or characterised by a 
dominant visual signal. The Dynamic Signal Calibration Module 180 may comprise 
an Achromatic Signal Gate 181 and a Dominant Signal Contextualiser 182. The 
30 
Dynamic Signal Calibration Module 180 may maintain dual attribution records 
11 
storing both an unmodified raw attribution vector and a distinct context-calibrated 
attribution vector along with thresholds, model version and selected analysis mode. 
[0049] 
A Design-Prescription Generator 190 may select a modifiable feature 
with an adverse calibrated contribution and compute a bounded change. The 
bounded change may include reduction of stroke weight, increase of inter-character 
5 
spacing, adjustment of circularity, reduction of edge-density imbalance, or 
constrained palette adjustment. 
[0050] 
A Candidate Modification Module 200 may construct and re-evaluate 
bounded logo variations. The Candidate Modification Module 200 may evaluate 
candidate changes Δx and select the minimum change satisfying the improvement 
10 
criterion T̂(x+Δx) − T̂(x) ≥ δ and applicable design-preservation constraints. 
[0051] 
A Reporting and Visualisation Module 210 may produce output 
including the trust-propensity score, active analysis mode, raw and calibrated 
explanations, design prescription and before/after comparison report. 
[0052] 
An Audit and Provenance Store 220 may retain input metadata, 
15 
extracted features, raw and calibrated attributions, thresholds, model version, 
selected mode and output history for auditability. The Audit and Provenance Store 
220 receives data from the Dynamic Signal Calibration Module 180, the Design-
Prescription Generator 190, the Candidate Modification Module 200 and the 
Reporting and Visualisation Module 210.

[0055] 
The method may begin with logo reception and validation at the Image

30 
may receive and validate a logo image or a baseline/revision pair, verifying file

[0056]

[0057] 
The method may proceed with multi-domain feature extraction at the

10

[0058] 
When a matched pair is supplied, the method may proceed with

The method may proceed with colour-to-emotion mapping at the

20 
[0060] 
The method may proceed with trust-propensity generation at the Trust-
Inference Engine 160. The Trust-Inference Engine 160 may generate a normalised

[0061] 
The method may optionally proceed with ensembling at the Ensemble

25

[0062] 
The method may proceed with raw attribution retention at the

feature-attribution values for the current prediction and retain the raw attribution 
30 
vector for subsequent processing and audit purposes. 
13 
[0063] 
The method may proceed with chromatic condition classification and

180. The Dynamic Signal Calibration Module 180 may classify the chromatic

5 
activate when measured saturation S is below the achromatic threshold τa to

Contextualiser 182 may activate when a dominance ratio D exceeds the dominance

10 
[0064] 
The method may proceed with modifiable feature selection and

[0065] 
The method may optionally proceed with candidate logo construction 
15 
and re-evaluation at the Candidate Modification Module 200. The Candidate

20 
[0066] 
The method may conclude with output of score, explanations,

[0067] 
FIG. 3 illustrates the Dynamic Signal Calibration Module 180 
producing a context-calibrated attribution vector from a raw attribution vector 
generated by the Explainability Engine 170 according to various embodiments. The 
30 
Dynamic Signal Calibration Module 180 evaluates measured saturation S and 
14 
dominance ratio D against configurable thresholds to determine the appropriate 
processing pathway. 
[0068] 
The Achromatic Signal Gate 181 may activate when measured 
saturation S is below an achromatic threshold τa. In some cases, the Achromatic 
Signal Gate 181 may use an illustrative laboratory threshold of τa = 0.05 for 
5 
detecting non-operative colour conditions. When the Achromatic Signal Gate 181 
activates, colour-domain entries may be set to zero only in the prescription vector 
Φcal while preserving the raw attribution vector Φraw. The Achromatic Signal Gate 
181 excludes colour features from the prescription pathway, redirecting the 
diagnostic process to geometry, typography and structure while avoiding irrelevant 
10 
palette advice for monochromatic or near-monochromatic logos. 
[0069] 
When saturation S exceeds the achromatic threshold τa, the Dynamic 
Signal Calibration Module 180 may evaluate whether the dominance ratio D 
exceeds a dominance threshold τd. The Dominant Signal Contextualiser 182 may 
activate when the dominance ratio D exceeds the threshold τd. In some cases, the 
15 
Dominant Signal Contextualiser 182 may use an illustrative dominance threshold 
of τd = 0.40 for detecting dominant visual signals. 
[0070] 
When the Dominant Signal Contextualiser 182 activates, the raw 
attribution vector Φraw may be retained and a contextual preservation/modification 
score ψ may be generated without altering the raw attribution. The Dominant Signal 
20 
Contextualiser 182 may compute the contextual score ψ = h(Φraw, D, model 
consistency, optional preservation constraints) to determine whether the dominant 
signal is preserved, cautiously modified, or modified. The contextual score enables 
intentional dominant brand colours to be recognised and handled appropriately 
rather than being flagged as defects. 
25 
[0071] 
When the dominance ratio D does not exceed the threshold τd and 
saturation S exceeds the achromatic threshold τa, the Dynamic Signal Calibration 
Module 180 may apply standard chromatic interpretation. In the standard chromatic 
interpretation pathway, the raw attribution values may be processed without 
achromatic gating or dominant-signal contextualisation. 
30
